🔄 Synchronous Machines

EMF equation & winding factors, voltage regulation, power-angle equation, and power-factor correction.

1. Principle & the EMF equation

A DC-excited rotor (field) spins at synchronous speed ns = 120f/p, sweeping its flux past a distributed stator winding and inducing a 3-phase EMF. Because the winding is spread over several slots per pole per phase and often uses a "short-pitched" (fractional-pitch) coil, the coil voltages don't add up perfectly in phase — two correction factors are needed:

kd = sin(qα/2) / [q·sin(α/2)]     kp = sin(β·90°/τ)     kw = kdkp

where q = slots/pole/phase = Q/(pm), α = slot angle = 180°/(mq), τ = full pole pitch (slots), β = actual coil pitch (slots).

Erms (per phase) = 4.44 · kw · f · N · φm

where N = turns/phase (= half the series conductors/phase) and φm = peak flux/pole.

If the flux has harmonic content (e.g. 3rd harmonic), each harmonic needs its OWN distribution factor (using α×harmonic order) and its own EMF term; total rms voltage is the root-sum-square of the fundamental and harmonic rms voltages — this matters because 3rd-harmonic line voltages actually cancel in a star connection, which is one reason star connections are preferred.

2. Synchronous impedance & voltage regulation

Per phase, the machine is modelled as an internal EMF V0 behind synchronous impedance Zs=Ra+jXs, feeding the terminal voltage Vt:

V0 = Vt + Ia(Ra + jXs)   (generator, using load-current sign convention)
Percent regulation = (V0 − Vt)/Vt × 100%

Work this out as a phasor sum: put Vt on the real axis, write Ia at the load's power-factor angle (lagging → negative angle, leading → positive angle), multiply by Zs, add to Vt, then take the magnitude.

Load typeTypical regulation
Lagging (inductive) pfLarge and positive — voltage sags significantly under load
Unity pfSmall positive
Leading (capacitive) pfSmall, or even negative — voltage can rise under load
Worked example

230 V (line), wye, Xs=1.2 Ω, Ra=0.5 Ω, full load 0.8 lagging pf, rated 10 kVA. Find % regulation.

  1. Vt(phase) = 230/√3 = 132.8 V. Ia = 10000/(√3×230) = 25.1 A at 0.8 lag → 20.08−j15.06 A.
  2. V0 = √[(Vtcosφ+IaRa)² + (Vtsinφ+IaXs)²] using the phasor law directly = √[(106.24+12.55)²+(79.68+30.12)²] ≈ 161.76 V.
  3. Regulation = (161.76−132.8)/132.8 × 100 ≈ 21.8%.

Repeating with 0.8 leading pf (same machine) gives V₀≈128.7V, i.e. regulation ≈ −3.1% — notice the sign flips.

3. The power-angle equation & operating modes

For a round-rotor machine (Ra negligible), the power transferred between terminal voltage and internal EMF, separated by power (torque) angle δ, is:

P (per phase) = (Vt·V0/Xs)·sin δ

For a salient-pole machine (Xd ≠ Xq), an extra "reluctance power" term appears:

P = (VtV0/Xd)sin δ + (Vt²/2)(1/Xq − 1/Xd)sin 2δ
  • δ > 0 (V0 leads Vt) → generator action (delivering power out).
  • δ < 0 (V0 lags Vt) → motor action (absorbing power in).
This power-angle view (from the machine's own internal geometry) is exactly equivalent to the induction-machine-style "power ∝ sin(angle between rotor and stator MMF)" picture — same physics, two ways of writing it, as your study guide notes (FKU Eq. 4.73 vs 4.65/5.1).

4. Synchronous motor & power-factor correction

As a motor, the machine's power factor is set by its excitation (field current), not by the mechanical load:

  • Under-excited → V0 < needed → motor draws lagging current (absorbs vars, behaves inductively).
  • Over-excited → V0 > needed → motor draws leading current (supplies vars, behaves capacitively).
  • Exactly the right excitation gives unity pf and minimum armature current for a given power.

This is exploited for power-factor correction: run an over-excited synchronous motor in parallel with lagging loads (like induction motors); it supplies leading vars that cancel out some of the lagging vars, raising the overall system power factor — while also doing useful mechanical work, unlike a static capacitor bank.

Worked example — PF correction

An induction motor takes 350 kW at 0.707 lagging. A synchronous motor is added, taking 150 kW, and the combined pf becomes 0.9 lagging. Find the synchronous motor's kVA rating.

  1. Induction motor: S=350/0.707=495 kVA, Q=495sin(45°)=−350 kvar (lagging, i.e. absorbing).
  2. Combined: P=500kW, S=500/0.9=555.5kVA, Q=555.5sin(cos⁻¹0.9)=−242.16 kvar.
  3. Synchronous motor's own Q = −242.16−(−350)=107.84 kvar (it is supplying/leading). Its kVA = √(150²+107.84²) ≈ 184.7 kVA.
