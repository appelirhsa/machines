🌀 Polyphase Induction Motors

Rotating field & slip, equivalent circuit, torque, testing, and starting methods.

1. The rotating field & slip

A balanced 3-phase stator winding produces a magnetic field that rotates at synchronous speed:

ns = 120f / p   (rpm, p = number of poles)

The rotor is dragged along by this field but can never quite catch it (otherwise there'd be no relative motion, no induced rotor EMF, and no torque). The difference is slip:

s = (ns − n)/ns      n = (1−s)ns

The rotor "sees" the field slip past it at frequency f2 = s·f1, so the induced rotor EMF and current are at this slip frequency — very high (≈ f₁) at standstill, and very low near synchronous speed.

The rotor's own magnetic field always rotates at synchronous speed relative to the stator — whatever the slip — which is exactly why a constant torque is produced (the two fields must be stationary relative to each other for steady torque).

2. Per-phase equivalent circuit & power flow

Just like a transformer, but with the rotor circuit's resistance divided by slip:

Ze = R1 + jX1 + (jXm ∥ (R2/s + jX2))

Power flow across the machine, per phase (Pg = air-gap power = power crossing into the rotor circuit):

Pg = 3I2²(R2/s)     Rotor Cu loss = s·Pg     Pdeveloped = (1−s)Pg

Shaft output power = Pdeveloped − mechanical (friction & windage) losses. Motor efficiency = shaft output / total electrical input (stator input = Pg + stator Cu loss + stator core loss).

A very common trap: rotor core loss is normally neglected because rotor frequency is so low — but stator core loss is NOT neglected, since the stator always sees full supply frequency.

3. Torque & maximum torque

Te = Pg/ωs = (3I2²R2) / (s·ωs)

Differentiating torque with respect to R2 (holding s and X2 fixed) shows maximum torque occurs when:

R2 = s·X2   (at standstill, s=1: R2=X2 gives max starting torque)

A handy ratio form, useful when you know Tmax and the slip s* at which it occurs, and want torque at any other slip s:

Te/Tmax = 2(s*/s) / [1 + (s*/s)²]
This is why wound-rotor (slip-ring) machines can add external rotor resistance to shift the slip at which maximum torque occurs — pushing it towards standstill for a high starting torque, without the very high starting current of a low-resistance cage rotor.

4. No-load & blocked-rotor tests

TestAnalogous toFinds
No-load testTransformer open-circuit testMagnetising branch (Rm, Xm) — slip ≈ 0 so R2/s is huge, negligible current flows there
Blocked-rotor test (reduced voltage)Transformer short-circuit testSeries (leakage) impedance Re=R1+a²R2, Xe=X1+a²X2 — s=1 so R2/s is small, almost all current flows through the series branch

From the no-load test: Rm=V₀²/P₀, Xm=V₀²/√((V₀I₀)²−P₀²). From the blocked-rotor test: Re=Ps/Is², Xe=√((VsIs)²−Ps²)/Is².

Worked example

No-load: 400 V(L-L), 1770 W, 18.5 A. Friction & windage = 600 W. Blocked-rotor: 45 V(L-L), 2700 W, 63 A. Find the approximate equivalent circuit.

  1. No-load per-phase: V₀=231V, P₀=(1770−600)/3=390W, I₀=18.5A → Rm=231²/390=136.8Ω, Xm=231²/√(231²·18.5²−390²)=12.5Ω.
  2. Blocked-rotor per-phase: Vs=25.98V, Is=63A, Ps=900W → Re=900/63²=0.23Ω, Xe=√(25.98²·63²−900²)/63²=0.34Ω.

5. Starting methods

MethodEffect on starting currentEffect on starting torque
Direct-on-line (DOL)Full — typically 5–9× full-load currentFull rated starting torque
Star-delta starterReduced to ≈1/3 of DOL valueReduced to ≈1/3 of DOL value (both scale with V², and star gives V/√3 per phase)
Autotransformer at tap ratio kReduced to k × (DOL current)Reduced to k² × (DOL torque) — current only drops by k on the supply side, but torque (∝V²) drops by k²
Wound-rotor + external rotor resistanceReduced smoothly as resistance is cut out in stagesCan actually be increased at standstill — pushes max-torque slip towards s=1
Soft starter (voltage ramp) / VSD (V/Hz control)Controlled, low inrushControlled, smooth acceleration; a VSD can maintain full torque capability throughout
Autotransformer starting torque ratio: Ts/TFL = (Is/IFL)²·sFL, where Is is the actual (reduced) starting current on the motor side = k × (full-voltage DOL starting current).
Fuse / protection sizing (a favourite in your past papers): compute the actual full-load or per-phase running current from the rated power, voltage, connection (star/delta) and efficiency/pf, then size the fuse with the required overrating (commonly asked as +20%) to allow for the higher starting current without nuisance-tripping on normal running.
